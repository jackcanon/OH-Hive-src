## Summary

Same 70B three-way placement as R3b, benchmarked at 0 / 25 / 50 / 100 ms of one-way delay added to one of the two worker hops (Heimdall). Decode fell 6.67 -> 5.51 -> 4.87 -> 3.88 tok/s; per token 150 -> 181 -> 205 -> 258 ms, i.e. **each token pays roughly 1.1x the injected one-way delay** - one trip through the slow leg per token, exactly the model from the D17 research (Petals: 100 ms RTT ~ 30-70% slower; here -42% at 100 ms on one hop of two). Prefill fell less (67.9 -> 47.4, -30%) because a 512-token batch amortizes the round trip. The 0 ms baseline reproduced R3b to the hundredth (67.92/6.67 vs 67.87/6.67). Netem was cleared after every step; Heimdall RTT back to 0.32 ms at the end.

**Second finding, arguably the bigger one: shard distribution collapses with latency.** Uploading Heimdall's 9 GB share took ~3 min at 0 ms, ~6.5 at 25, ~10 at 50, and ~15 at 100 ms (observed ~11 MB/s at 100 ms vs ~110 MB/s on the LAN) - single-stream TCP throughput vs round-trip time. Total step wall time 5:16 -> 18:00. On the open internet a 9 GB shard would take a quarter of an hour to arrive over one connection before a single token is generated.

## What this means for Halo v2 (lesson L7)

1. Decode cost over WAN is predictable: ms/token ≈ LAN ms/token + Σ(one-way delay of each hop) x ~1.1. A member 50 ms away on a two-hop pool costs ~55 ms per token; three members at 100 ms each would put a 70B near 1-2 tok/s. The scheduler can price latency exactly like it prices bandwidth (L2): each node's cost = share ÷ bandwidth + its RTT to the host.
2. Prefill tolerates latency far better than decode. Long-prompt, short-output jobs (summarize, classify, extract) are the WAN-friendly shape; long generations are not.
3. Shard distribution is the WAN bottleneck before inference even starts. v2 needs parallel/chunked shard transfer (multiple TCP streams or QUIC), shard caching on workers across jobs (llama.cpp's `-c` cache, or Halo's own), and pre-placement of popular models - otherwise every job pays minutes of upload per remote member.
4. Variance grows with latency (pp512 ±0.07 -> ±1.89). Jitter, not just RTT, will matter on real links; Test 03 should add `netem delay 50ms 20ms` (jitter) and loss.
